Multistructure of the Universe

A theoretical model describing our Space-Time Continuum (STC) as a temporary, discrete medium formed at the intersection of two external primordial structures, and an open record of how the model holds up against known data and fundamental laws.

Author of the model: Viacheslav Areshchenko, Kyiv, Ukraine Version: revised 8 November 2026, after systematic verification

Abstract

The STC model (in Ukrainian: ПЧК, просторово-часовий континуум) describes space-time as a discrete medium made of two nested sublattices (“a grid within a grid”). The medium arose at the intersection of two clouds of elementary constituents, called amers, after a collision of two primordial entities, A and B. In November 2026 the model was systematically checked against measured quantities and fundamental laws; all of the author's formulas were recalculated numerically.

🟢 Consistent with data

The STC structure coincides with the Yee lattice; a medium with ε = μ = 1 + 2GM/(rc²) reproduces general-relativistic light bending (ratio to Newtonian 2.004); Planck's constant is h = mS·c·lS; quadrature oscillation of two sublattices accounts for ħ, the sign of charge, antiparticles and spin ½; lattice dispersion gives de Broglie waves and time dilation; force balance at the Bohr radius gives −13.6 eV.

🔵 Predictions

The predicted dark-energy parameter wa agrees with DESI DR2 within ~1σ; dark energy never becomes phantom (w ≥ −1); the proton is absolutely stable, to be tested by Hyper-Kamiokande.

🔴 Not confirmed

Several earlier formulas depend on human units (coulomb, metre); the pressure-dependent proton radius; a head-on, axisymmetric collision as the source of CMB anomalies. The main open task is to derive α and the scale Le from the mechanics of the lattice.

2. Core Hypothesis

This section states the author's positions, without assessment.

The force that initiated and continues to shape our Universe is located outside our Space-Time Continuum. The collision of two primordial entities, A and B, produced two dense, “hot” clouds of amers of different kinds. Our STC is the region where these clouds intersect.

BM A

The first primordial structure, a cloud of type-A amers. It exerts its own pressure, Pa, on the STC. The ratio of mass-energy of the two outer structures is MA/MB ≈ 1836, the same as the proton-to-electron mass ratio.

BM B

The second primordial structure, a cloud of type-B amers. It exerts pressure Pb and interacts with BM A in the overlapping region. The author also associates the two structures with dark-matter and dark-energy components 🟡 interpretation, not yet tested.

2.1. The Universe as a temporary lens

The intersection of the two clouds has the shape of an asymmetric lens and expands under the mutual pressure of the clouds. Energy is conserved in the whole system “two clouds + STC”. The Universe is therefore not assumed to be eternal: it is born, evolves, and eventually dissolves as the interaction changes.

$$\text{Structure A} + \text{Structure B} \longrightarrow \text{STC lens (Universe)} \longrightarrow \text{decay into the initial structures}$$

2.2. Inner structure: a grid within a grid

The STC consists of two nested cubic sublattices of the CsCl type: the nodes of one sublattice sit at the centres of the cells of the other. The sublattices are built of type-A and type-B amers respectively. A photon is an oscillation of this lattice: an excitation of one sublattice passes to the other. The speed of light is the geometric mean of the propagation speeds in the two sublattices, $c = \sqrt{v_1 v_2}$.

2.3. The cell of the STC is the Stoney cell

The cell size is $l_S = 1.18880 \times 10^{-33}$ m and its mass is $m_S = \sqrt{\alpha}\, m_P = 1.8592 \times 10^{-9}$ kg. The amers inside it are extremely small, but “small only for us”: only dimensionless ratios carry physical meaning.

2.4. Time as the rate of structural change

Time is not an absolute container but the rate at which the structure of the STC changes. Where the structure barely changes (near a black hole), time almost stops. When pressure changes, time changes with it, which is why the fine-structure constant α stays constant.

2.5. Principle of universality

Different civilisations use different units, so only dimensionless ratios have physical meaning. A formula is treated as physical if its result does not change when metres are replaced by centimetres or feet, and if it does not contain the numerical value of the coulomb (1 C = 6.2415 × 10¹⁸ elementary charges, a human “dozen”). The universal number for charge is α = 1/137.036.

5. Results: the Atom and Matter Waves

The picture “the electron oscillates, it does not orbit” reproduces the hydrogen atom exactly, and the dispersion of the STC lattice yields the de Broglie relation and time dilation.

5.1. Force balance in hydrogen 🟢

In the ground state the orbital angular momentum is l = 0: there is no rotation. The attraction αħc/r² and the quantum pressure ħ²/(m·r³) are exactly equal at r = a₀, both 8.23872 × 10⁻⁸ N. The energy at equilibrium is −13.606 eV; the frequency of small oscillations ħ/(m·a₀²) = 4.134 × 10¹⁶ rad/s matches the Bohr frequency.

5.2. Geometric meaning of α 🟢

The author's quantity 2π·α·a₀ equals the Compton wavelength of the electron, λC = 2.42631 × 10⁻¹² m. Hence α = ƛC/a₀: the ratio of the size of the electron's own sphere to the region of its oscillation.

5.3. Repulsion as a buoyancy force 🟢 🟡

If the compression energy ħ²/(2m·r²) is stored as pressure inside the Compton sphere, the force F = −V·∇P gives exactly ħ²/(m·r³), and with it a₀ and −13.6 eV. This step is equivalent to the standard calculation and shows that the picture is consistent; the electron oscillates around the point where this push balances the attraction, like an Archimedes force.

5.4. Lattice wave equation

A node feels its coupling to its neighbours and a restoring force at its own site. In the continuum limit this is the Klein–Gordon equation:

$$\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2\nabla^2 u - \omega_0^2 u, \qquad \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + c^2k^2, \qquad \omega_0 = \frac{mc^2}{\hbar}$$

This dispersion gives E² = (mc²)² + (pc)², p = γmv, λ = h/(γmv), and an internal clock rate ω₀/γ. Numerical check for the electron:

6. Expansion, Dark Energy and External Pressure

In standard cosmology, accelerated expansion is modelled through the cosmological constant $\Lambda$ or more general dark-energy models. In this model, the same phenomenon is interpreted as the internal manifestation of the pressure exerted by the two external clouds on the STC lens.

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$$

This is a conceptual reinterpretation, not a replacement for the mathematical formulation of general relativity. The minimal model of lens expansion gives a specific equation of state for dark energy:

$$w(a) = -1 + \frac{2}{3}\,\frac{a^2}{1-a^2}$$

Using w₀ from DESI DR2, the predicted wa agrees with the measured value at ~1.0σ (Pantheon+), ~0.3σ (Union3) and ~0.8σ (DESY5). 🟢 🟡

Caveats: the expansion model is minimal and does not yet include the w₀–wa correlation or the matter contribution; the “thawing” class of dark-energy models is not unique to the STC.

8.1. The neutron as a temporary state

The neutron is not a mechanical union of a proton and an electron but a new, excited, temporary “tight pair”. Its formation and decay must include the neutrino; without it, lepton number and angular momentum are not conserved:

$$p^+ + e^- \longrightarrow n^0 + \nu_e, \qquad n^0 \longrightarrow p^+ + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e$$

The macro-analogy of the model treats the Universe itself as such a temporary state of two more fundamental structures:

$$\text{BM A} + \text{BM B} \longrightarrow \text{Universe} \longrightarrow \text{BM A} + \text{BM B}$$

This analogy is a structural metaphor, not a literal equivalence between nuclear physics and cosmology. The earlier version of this page wrote p⁺ + e⁻ → n⁰ without the neutrino; this has been corrected.

9. Multiverse Landscape and Causal Isolation

The model allows other temporary lenses formed by other interactions between primordial structures. Such worlds may exist in parallel but remain causally inaccessible. 🟡 not testable at present

Simple short-lived worlds

Low-energy interactions may form simple lenses that collapse quickly or never develop stable matter, stars or complex structures.

Complex long-lived worlds

Interactions with an optimal energy balance may form long-lived universes with stable constants, matter, stars, planets and potentially life.

This leads to an anthropic interpretation: we observe a complex Universe because only such a Universe allows observers to appear and ask questions about its origin.

10. Life Cycle of the STC

The model describes a finite cosmological episode rather than an eternal universe. The sequence below is the author's scenario; its late stages are a hypothesis 🟡 with one testable consequence: dark energy should never become phantom (see Predictions).

Interaction

Two primordial entities collide; two clouds of amers form and their pressure fields begin to overlap.

Formation

The STC lens appears in the intersection zone. Inside it this is experienced as the origin of the Universe.

Condensation

A one-time condensation of p + e⁻ pairs, photons and neutrons (with neutrinos) fills the STC with matter and radiation.

Expansion

The lens expands under the mutual pressure of the clouds. Galaxies, stars and complex structures emerge.

Anisotropic collapse

The collapse begins along the line of the primordial pressure (the “Axis of Evil”), not symmetrically in all directions.

Final state and phase transition

The STC collapses into a single black-hole-like state; its entropy is reset to zero, and it dissolves back into the primordial structures. No internal trace is preserved.

14. Open Problems and Work Plan

The main task is to move from identities to derivations: to obtain α and the scale Le from the mechanics of the STC lattice.

Priority 1: before publication

  • Verify against primary sources the numbers quoted from memory: proton-radius measurements, proton polarisability, limits on the maximum speed of matter.
  • Replace copies of Planck and WMAP data with the official Planck Legacy Archive and LAMBDA files and repeat the low-multipole analysis.
  • Include the w₀–wa correlation and the matter contribution in the comparison with DESI.

Priority 2: theory

  • Define Le without the charge e (lattice step, frequencies fA, fB and pressure P in the author's formula R = m·fA·fB/P).
  • Derive α as a ratio of impedances, α = Z₀/(2RK), from the mechanics of the two sublattices.
  • Show that quadrature oscillation A + B creates only an E field (no magnetic monopoles), and what fixes the π/2 phase shift.
  • Explain photon spin 1 alongside electron spin ½.
  • Choose lattice couplings so that the optical branch has the right sign of curvature and the same limiting speed c.
  • Calculate the Darwin term for the Compton sphere in the STC.
  • Explain the proton radius of 0.84 fm (the law R = m/K gives 0.608 fm).

Priority 3: cosmology

  • The law of pressure change PA(t), PB(t) and the nature of the entities' motion (head-on or oblique).
  • The damping of the lens-boundary influence with multipole l.
  • The latent heat of condensation: why ~1.6 × 10⁹ photons per proton; why ~3 neutrino species.
  • Acoustic peaks and the BAO scale (~147 Mpc); isotropy at 10⁻⁵ despite an asymmetric lens.
  • The cosmological-constant problem: why an STC density of ~10⁹⁶ kg/m³ does not gravitate (uniformity, external pressure of the clouds).
A model involving structures outside the observable STC becomes scientific only through observable consequences inside our Universe. The decisive next step is to derive α = Z₀/(2RK) as a ratio of the impedances of the two sublattices. If this succeeds, the model will predict a fundamental constant for the first time.

15. Conclusion

In its current form the STC model does not contradict any tested conservation law and reproduces a number of exact relations of physics. But most of its numerical formulas are still identities, not derivations.

  1. Strongest results: the Yee-lattice structure; quadrature oscillation of two sublattices as the source of ħ, charge and spin ½; lattice dispersion yielding de Broglie waves and time dilation; condensation of p + e⁻ pairs explaining the equality of charges and the absence of antimatter.
  2. Quantitative agreement with data: light bending, GPS time dilation, the hydrogen atom, DESI wa within ~1σ, rim modes for l = 2, 3 (p ≈ 0.01).
  3. Main weakness: formulas that depend on the coulomb and the metre, and the fact that α and Le are not yet derived from lattice mechanics.
  4. Nearest experimental tests: Hyper-Kamiokande (proton stability), future DESI releases (w ≥ −1), LiteBIRD (polarisation at l = 2, 3).

The philosophical implication of the model is severe: humanity and the observable cosmos are not permanent features of reality, but a temporary and beautiful flash within a larger, unknown geometry. Its scientific value will be decided by the predictions above.
